Based on the NALT Citation Guide, here's how to format citations for chapters in edited books, which should help you in building your citation generator tool:
When citing a particular chapter in a book edited by one or more people, you should include the following elements in the footnote:
•
Author(s) of the Chapter: Cite the author’s surname with initials. If there are up to three authors, insert ‘and’ between their names.
•
‘Title of the Chapter (in single quotes)’: Enclose the title of the chapter within single quotation marks. The first letter of all major words in the title should be capitalised.
•
‘in’: This word precedes the editor(s) information.
•
Editor(s) of the Book: Provide the name(s) of the editor(s) followed by the designation ‘(ed)’ for one editor or ‘(eds)’ for multiple editors.
•
‘Title of the Book (italicized)’: The title of the book should be in italics.
•
Publisher: Include the name of the publisher.
•
Year of Publication: Provide the year of publication, usually enclosed in brackets along with the publisher.
•
Page Range of the Chapter: Indicate the page range of the specific chapter you are citing.
Here is an example from the source:
•
David Jangkam, ‘The Value of Authorship in the Digital Environment’ in Clement Francis Kwede and Karen Shaakaa (eds), World Wide Research: Reshaping the Sciences and Humanities in the Century of Information (MIT, 2010) 62.
And another example with a page range:
•
David Jangkam, ‘The Value of Authorship in the Digital Environment’ in Clement Francis Kwede and Karen Shaakaa (eds), World Wide Research: Reshaping the Sciences and Humanities in the Century of Information (MIT 2010) 62-80.
For bibliography entries, the format is slightly different, with the author's surname preceding the initials, and generally, page numbers for the chapter are omitted. For example:
•
Jangkam D, ‘The Value of Authorship in the Digital Environment’ in Kwede CF and Shaakaa K (eds), World Wide Research: Reshaping the Sciences and Humanities in the Century of Information (MIT, 2010).
