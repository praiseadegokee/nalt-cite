The citation generator will be designed with a user-friendly interface that allows users to select the type of source they wish to cite. This selection will then dynamically display the relevant input fields for that source type. The tool will cater to a wide range of legal and academic materials as outlined in the NALT guide.
Here's a breakdown of the key features and their granular input fields for different source categories:
•	Core Functionality:
o	Source Type Selection: A dropdown menu or a similar mechanism allowing users to choose from various source types such as Cases, Statutes, Books, Journal Articles, Online Sources, Conference Papers, Newspapers/Magazines, Projects/Theses, Inaugural/Distinguished Lectures, Encyclopedias, Monographs, and Lecture Notes.
o	Automated Footnote Generation: The primary output will be in the NALT's footnote style of referencing, using Arabic numerals in superscript within the text as citation indicators, with the full citation appearing at the foot of the page.
o	Minimal Punctuation: The tool will automatically apply the NALT's principle of minimal use of punctuation marks, such as omitting full stops after abbreviations (e.g., LFN, NWLR).
o	'v' for 'versus': In case citations, the tool will use an unpunctuated italicized 'v' to separate the names of the parties (e.g., Okoye v Lagos State Govt).
o	Italicization: The tool will automatically apply italicization where required, such as for the names of cases and the titles of journals, newspapers, etc..
o	Bibliography Generation (Potential): While the primary focus is footnotes, the tool could potentially offer an option to generate a bibliography. It should account for the differences between footnotes and bibliography entries, such as the order of author names (surname first in bibliography) and the general omission of page numbers in the bibliography.
•	Author Input:
o	Multiple Author Support: The tool will allow users to add multiple authors for a single source through an "Add Author" feature.
o	Individual Author Fields: For each individual author, there will be separate fields for: 
	First Name
	Middle Name(s)
	Last Name/Surname
o	Organizational Author Field: A distinct field will be available for specifying the name of an organizational author (e.g., Nigerian Association of Law Teachers) [Our conversation].

o	For Cases:
	Names of Parties: Separate fields for the plaintiff(s) and defendant(s).
	Year of Reporting (in brackets):.

	Page Number(s) or Range:.
	Unreported Status (Checkbox): To indicate if the case is unreported [Our conversation].
	Court Information (for Unreported Cases): Fields for the name of the court and the date of judgment (e.g., Federal High Court Jos Judicial Division, 17 June 2016) [Our conversation].
o	For Statutes (including Constitution, Acts, Rules of Court, Practice Directions):
	Title of the Statute: Full title (e.g., Constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria, Freedom of Information Act).
	Year of Enactment/Making: (e.g., 1999, 2011, 2016).
